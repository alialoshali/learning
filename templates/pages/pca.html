Why it exists: real data has many features: an image has thousands of pixels, a word embedding has hundreds of numbers. Most of these features are redundant and move together. PCA finds the few directions along which the data really varies, so we can describe each example with far fewer numbers, visualise it in 2-D, remove noise, and speed up later models. It is also the clearest way to understand projections and low-rank matrices, two ideas that appear throughout Transformers.

01The idea: find the best line to project onto

Suppose every point is described by two features that are strongly related, such as height and weight. Most of the information is in one direction: “overall size”. PCA asks: onto which line should we project the data to keep as much of it as possible?

Two ways to say “as much as possible”, and they give the same answer:

  1. Maximum variance: the projected points (orange) should be as spread out as possible.
  2. Minimum reconstruction error: the perpendicular gaps (grey lines) between each point and its projection should be as short as possible.

Why are they the same? For a centred point, Pythagoras gives \(\lVert x\rVert^2=(\text{projection})^2+(\text{gap})^2\). The left side is fixed, so making the projection large makes the gap small.

$$\underbrace{\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum\lVert x_i\rVert^2}_{\text{total variance (fixed)}}=\underbrace{\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum (u^\top x_i)^2}_{\text{variance kept}}+\underbrace{\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum\lVert x_i-uu^\top x_i\rVert^2}_{\text{reconstruction error}}$$

Try it: rotate the line by hand and try to maximise the orange bar. Then press Find best. The winning direction is the first principal component, PC1.

02The covariance matrix: how features vary together

All the information PCA needs is in one \(d\times d\) matrix. First centre the data (subtract the mean \(\bar x\)); then:

$$\Sigma=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_i-\bar x)(x_i-\bar x)^\top=\frac{1}{N-1}X_c^\top X_c$$
\(\Sigma_{jj}\)diagonal: the variance of feature \(j\) (its spread)
\(\Sigma_{jk}\)off-diagonal: the covariance of features \(j\) and \(k\). Positive = they rise together, negative = one rises as the other falls, 0 = no linear relation
\(X_c\)the centred data matrix, one row per example (\(N\times d\))

The variance of the data along any unit direction \(u\) is \(u^\top\Sigma u\). So PCA is the problem:

$$\max_{\lVert u\rVert=1}\ u^\top\Sigma\,u$$

Try it: change the spreads and the correlation \(\rho\). The matrix updates, and the ellipse (2 standard deviations) tilts. At \(\rho=0\) the off-diagonal is 0 and the ellipse lines up with the axes.

Transformer link\(X^\top X\) is a matrix of dot products between features; attention’s \(QK^\top\) is a matrix of dot products between tokens. Both are Gram matrices, a table of all pairwise similarities.

03Eigenvectors: directions the matrix only stretches

A matrix transforms vectors: it usually rotates and stretches them. But some special directions are only stretched, not rotated. These are the eigenvectors, and the stretch factor is the eigenvalue:

$$\Sigma\,v=\lambda\,v$$

The solution of \(\max_{\lVert u\rVert=1}u^\top\Sigma u\) is the eigenvector with the largest eigenvalue, and the variance captured is exactly that \(\lambda\). The next component is the eigenvector with the second-largest \(\lambda\), and so on. For a covariance matrix, the eigenvectors are always perpendicular to each other.

Try it: turn the blue vector \(v\) around the circle. The orange arrow is \(\Sigma v\). Most of the time they point in different directions. Find the two angles where they line up: those are the eigenvectors. The circle of all unit vectors is mapped to the ellipse, whose axes are the eigenvectors.

In practiceLibraries compute PCA with the SVD (singular value decomposition) of \(X_c\) directly: \(X_c=USV^\top\). The columns of \(V\) are the principal directions and \(\lambda_k=s_k^2/(N-1)\).

04The algorithm in five steps

  1. Centre: subtract the mean of each feature (and usually standardise: divide by its standard deviation if features have different units).
  2. Covariance: \(\Sigma=X_c^\top X_c/(N-1)\).
  3. Eigen-decompose: \(\Sigma=V\Lambda V^\top\), with eigenvalues sorted \(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\dots\).
  4. Choose \(k\): keep the first \(k\) columns \(V_k\), e.g. enough to explain 95% of the variance: \(\sum_{j\le k}\lambda_j/\sum_j\lambda_j\ge0.95\).
  5. Project and, if needed, reconstruct:
    $$z=V_k^\top(x-\bar x)\in\mathbb R^k\qquad\hat x=\bar x+V_k\,z$$

Step through it on the right. Watch how step 5 is a rotation into the new axes followed by dropping the coordinates with little variance.

Transformer link\(z=V_k^\top x\) is a linear projection to a smaller space, exactly what the query, key and value matrices \(W_Q,W_K,W_V\) do in every attention head (\(d_{\text{model}}\to d_k\)). The difference is that PCA picks the directions by variance, whereas a Transformer learns them by gradient descent for its task.

05PCA on images: eigen-images and compression

Now real high-dimensional data: 400 small 14×14 grey images of simple shapes (blobs, boxes and bars at random positions). Each image is a vector with \(d=196\) numbers. PCA finds 196 orthogonal directions, each of which can itself be shown as a 14×14 image, an eigen-image (on faces these are called “eigenfaces”).

  • Scree plot (bottom): the eigenvalues fall quickly, so a few components hold most of the variance.
  • Reconstruction: any image ≈ mean image + \(z_1\cdot\)PC1 + \(z_2\cdot\)PC2 + … + \(z_k\cdot\)PC\(_k\).

Try it: raise \(k\) and watch the reconstruction sharpen. With \(k\approx25\) you keep most of the image using only about 13% of the numbers. Click Next image to try others.

Transformer linkResearchers run PCA on a Transformer’s hidden states to find what they encode, and often find that a few directions carry clear meanings (e.g. truth vs. falsehood, sentiment, position). This is a basic tool of interpretability.

06PCA for visualisation

We cannot look at 196-dimensional points. But we can plot every image by its first two PCA coordinates \((z_1,z_2)\). Colours show the true shape type, which PCA never saw.

Hover over points in different corners of the plot and look at the images. You will find that the axes mostly encode where the ink is (left vs. right, top vs. bottom), not the shape type, so the colours are mixed. Why? Moving a shape changes far more pixels than changing its shape, so position is where most of the variance lies.

Key lessonPCA keeps the directions of largest variance, which are not always the directions you care about. A model trained with labels (or a good self-supervised objective) can learn features that separate the shapes regardless of position. That is the motivation for CNNs and learned representations.

Limits of PCA: it is linear, so curved structure (like the moons in clustering) is flattened. Non-linear methods such as t-SNE and UMAP, or a neural autoencoder, can follow curves, at the cost of axes with no simple meaning.

07Low rank: why big weight matrices compress (→ LoRA)

The same idea applies to any matrix, not just data. The SVD writes a matrix as a sum of rank-1 pieces ordered by importance:

$$W=\sum_{j=1}^{r}s_j\,u_jv_j^\top$$$$W_k=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_j\,u_jv_j^\top\quad\text{(best rank-}k\text{ approximation)}$$

A \(d\times d\) matrix needs \(d^2\) numbers; a rank-\(k\) version needs only \(2dk\) (two thin matrices). If the singular values fall quickly, a small \(k\) is almost as good.

Try it: the 48×48 “weight matrix” on the left is approximated with rank \(k\) on the right. Watch the error and the parameter count.

Transformer linkLoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), the most common way to fine-tune LLMs cheaply, freezes the big weight \(W\) and learns only a low-rank update \(\Delta W=BA\) with \(B\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}\), \(A\in\mathbb R^{r\times d}\), \(r\approx8\). For \(d=4096\) that is 65k numbers instead of 16.8 million per matrix. Multi-head attention is also low rank: each head projects to \(d_k=d/h\) dimensions.

Teach it in one sentence: PCA rotates the data onto the orthogonal directions of greatest variance (the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix) and keeps only the top few, the best linear compression in the sense of least squared reconstruction error.
